Privacy Policy

PharmaSNAP AI · Last updated 16 October 2026

PharmaSNAP AI is an app that reads medicines and health documents from a photograph and explains them, out loud, in your language. It is made by LORDLY PTY LTD in Australia.

This policy explains what the app collects, which companies receive it, what it does not keep, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be read, not to be survived.

The short version. The app needs an account. To answer you, it sends what you give it to companies outside Australia, including in the United States. Photographs you scan, a PDF of blood-test results you choose, the questions you ask, and the saved health details and cabinet medicines that bear on a question go to Anthropic, whose AI models read them and write the answer. What you say into the microphone, and the words of an answer that is read aloud, go to OpenAI. Microsoft Azure is the backup for that voice work and runs the live conversation mode where it is switched on. Video capture and video imports are no longer available in the current app. In earlier versions, a short video you chose to send went to Google, with its sound and question, only after you agreed to send that clip. A pharmacy stock search sends the medicine name and the names of nearby pharmacies. We do not save scanned photographs, videos or chosen files in our own storage unless you choose to keep a cabinet photo. These companies can hold what they receive for a time under their own rules, as explained below. We keep your account, your cabinet, your dose records, saved chat text and what you add to My health until you delete them. We do not sell personal information. Paid pharmacy placements are labelled as sponsored.

Saved conversations

Questions, answers, their source references and the text read from a photograph or health document are saved privately with your account in Supabase so you can return to a conversation on another signed-in device. This can include sensitive health information. We do not save the raw photograph, video, PDF or microphone recording in chat history. Temporary media links and reading tokens are excluded. Old answers are a record of what was said then, not a fresh check of your health or medicine.

Open Saved chats from Home or a conversation to read, continue or delete a chat. Reading saved text does not call an AI model. A new follow-up question or newly generated speech can use your AI allowance. Only the relevant, bounded conversation context is sent with a follow-up; the full saved history is not sent automatically. Starting a new chat does not include other chats. Chats stay until you delete them or delete your account. Storage is limited to 1,000 chats and 50 MB per account, with a 2 MB limit per chat; the app shows a save error rather than silently deleting old chats. Signing out hides them on that device; it does not delete them.

The app may also keep a copy of an answer on your phone, so that the same question asked again can be answered at once without a new AI request. This is only for general questions about a medicine (what it is, what it is for, how it works, its side effects, how to store it), asked in English and without a photograph, video or file. Anything about a dose, timing, your health, another medicine, ingredients or a decision is always asked again. A saved copy is used for up to 7 days and is removed the next time you open the app after that. The question itself is not stored with the answer. An answer shown from the phone says "Saved answer" with the day it was saved, on the screen and aloud, and Ask again under it deletes the saved copy and asks afresh. Reading a saved answer aloud uses the voice service like any other answer, unless the audio is already held. These copies are kept in the app's own storage on that phone under your account. The app does not send them anywhere, another account on the same phone is never shown them, they stay on the phone when you sign out, and they are removed from the phone when you delete your account on it.

What we do not keep

The photographs you scan

When you photograph a medicine, a pharmacy label or a health document, the picture is sent to our server, which passes it to Anthropic to be read. We do not save the upload in our application storage. This does not mean every service provider deletes it immediately; provider retention is described below. The whole picture is sent, so a pharmacy label or a prescription goes with the name, the doctor and the pharmacy printed on it. The only photograph we store is one you deliberately save with a cabinet medicine. It is stored privately and is deleted when you delete that medicine or your account.

Video in earlier versions

Video capture and video imports are no longer available in the current app. The following explains how earlier versions handled clips, and remains relevant to people using those versions.

You can record or choose a clip of up to 15 seconds and 6 MB and ask a question about it. Before the clip is sent, the app asks you to agree, and it asks again for every new clip. If you agree, the whole clip and its sound pass through our server to Google Gemini, outside Australia. The question you typed or said is sent to Google with it, as text. Your photographs, your files, your microphone recordings and your saved health details are not sent to Google.

Google sends back a short description of what is seen and said in the clip. That description and your question go to Anthropic, which writes the answer. The app holds the description for up to two hours, tied to your account, so a follow-up question does not send the clip again.

We switch off Gemini's interaction history for these requests, and we do not store the original video in our application storage or in chat history. The question and the written answer are saved in your chat history like any other. Google may keep data for a time under its own service and abuse-monitoring terms; this is not a promise of immediate deletion.

Video questions are included with PharmaSnap Plus or an active trial, within the AI allowance. They are not included in Free outside an active trial. See the terms for current processing credits and refresh periods. A video answer can miss things that happen quickly. It does not tell you which medicine a loose tablet is, it does not tell you what is wrong with you, and it does not tell you whether the way you use a medicine or a device is safe.

A PDF of blood-test results you choose

You can also choose a PDF file of blood-test results, up to six pages, with "Choose a file". It is sent to our server and on to Anthropic to be read in the same way as a photograph, including every name, date of birth, Medicare number and other detail printed in it. We do not save the file in our application storage, and it is not kept with your account. Provider retention is described below. If the file is not a pathology report, the app says so and reports nothing from it.

Your location

When you ask for a pharmacy near you, your location is sent to our server so we can work out which pharmacies are closest. It is used for that search only: it is not saved to your account, not tracked in the background, and not given to the pharmacies.

What you say

When you ask a question by voice, the recording is sent to OpenAI to be turned into text, so the question can be answered. We do not keep the recording in our application storage. When the app reads an answer back to you, the words of that answer are sent to OpenAI too, to be turned into speech. Both of those happen on OpenAI's services outside Australia, including the United States.

Medicine reminders are kept on your phone only: the times you chose, the alert style and the medicine's name. They are not sent to our server. Alerts stop while you are signed out, and the reminders are removed from the phone when you delete your account. A spoken reminder plays a recording that is already inside the app. It says "your medicine", not the medicine's name, and nothing is sent to anyone to make it or to play it. The alert on your screen shows the medicine's name unless you turn that off.

Microsoft Azure does two things. It is our backup for the same voice work: a version of the app can be switched over to it, and then the recording and the words of the answer go to Azure instead of OpenAI. And there is a live conversation mode, switched on for some accounts, in which your phone streams the sound from your microphone straight to Azure for as long as the conversation is open.

Before the app uses any of this it asks you to agree to it, and you can leave the voice features alone if you would rather not.

Provider retention is separate from our application storage. We do not store your recordings, and the voice companies may keep them for up to 30 days under their own policies. OpenAI documents no application-state retention for its transcription and speech endpoints, but speech requests can be retained for abuse monitoring for up to thirty days under its default policy. Special account controls can differ. We do not claim that our account has zero data retention. See OpenAI's API data controls.

Shared medicine knowledge

When the shared knowledge feature is enabled, screened general medicine answers may be saved without account details to build PharmaSNAP's knowledge and improve future answers. This library excludes personal health information, personal questions, prescription directions and health documents. It is separate from the chats and health details kept privately in your account. Stored entries include the medicine, general question, language, sources and safety version, with no account identifier. Not every answer qualifies: it must pass the eligibility and answer screens. General library entries have no automatic expiry; they may be updated or removed when evidence or safety checks change.

When meaning matching is enabled, an eligible general medicine question may also be sent to OpenAI to calculate a numerical representation, called an embedding, to find relevant stored answers. No account identifier is included in that request. Personal questions are excluded from this feature. This builds the app's reference library; it is not a statement that your personal content is used to train an AI model. Photographs, files, recordings or question text sent for an answer can still contain names and other personal details you include, so we do not describe every AI request as anonymous.

Where your information goes

Most of what you send us is handled outside Australia, and you should know which parts before you send it.

  • Voice recordings, and the words of the answer read back to you, are handled by OpenAI in the United States and other OpenAI processing locations.
  • The photographs you scan, a PDF of blood-test results you choose to send, your questions, and the saved health details and cabinet medicines used to answer them, are handled by Anthropic in the United States and other Anthropic processing locations.
  • A short video you choose to send, its sound and the question you asked about it are handled by Google, outside Australia. This happens only for a video question, and only after you agree to send that clip. A description of the clip then goes to Anthropic with your question.
  • A pharmacy stock search runs a web search through Anthropic in the United States, and reads Australian retailers' own websites.
  • The backup voice service and the live conversation mode are handled by Microsoft Azure. Where that happens depends on how the service is set up, and it can be outside Australia.
  • Your account and everything saved in it are held by Supabase, our hosting provider, and can be held outside Australia. Purchases are handled by Apple and RevenueCat.

Their processing locations depend on the service and account settings. We cannot promise that any of this information stays in Australia, or that every provider processes it only in the United States. The app asks you to agree before it sends a photograph, a file, a question or a recording to these companies. It asks again, separately, before each new video is sent to Google.

You can take that permission back at any time, in the app: Profile → AI permission → Withdraw this permission. The app then goes back to the screen where you first agreed, and it cannot read a photo or answer a question until you allow it again. Your account and everything saved in it are kept. Taking the permission back does not delete what these companies have already received.

Looking for a pharmacy, and looking for stock

Two different things happen here, and only one of them touches the open web.

Finding the pharmacies near you is a lookup in our own directory, using the location you just gave us.

Asking whether a medicine is stocked nearby is a web search, where that check is switched on. To run it we send the name of the medicine, together with the names, suburbs and postcodes of the pharmacies nearest you, to two kinds of place: the big chains' own public websites, which we read directly, and Anthropic, whose AI model runs a web search for us. The chains are Australian; Anthropic is in the United States. We do not send your name, your email address, your exact location, your saved health details or the list of medicines in your cabinet. The pharmacies are not told that you asked.

A page on a website is not a shelf. What the app shows you is what a page said and when it said it, with a link, so you can ring the pharmacy and hear it from a person.

What a page said is kept, with no account attached: the pharmacy, the medicine, what the page said, the link and the time. For 24 hours it is used in place of a new check, so the same check is not run twice, and after that it is marked as older information. It is not private to the person who asked. Where stock checks are switched on for everyone, the app reads it back, for up to 90 days, for anyone signed in who looks up the same medicine near that pharmacy, with the time rounded to the hour. These records are not deleted after a set time, and deleting your account does not remove them, because nothing in them says who asked.

A pharmacy can pay for a labelled slot in the nearby list. It is marked as sponsored, it never changes the real distances or the order of the others, and we record that the slot was shown or tapped, with the distance and the time, so the sponsor can be billed for what was delivered. That record holds nothing about you, and nothing about you is sent to that pharmacy.

When the app shows a sponsor's logo, or a product picture from a pharmacy chain's website, your phone loads that picture from the website it is kept on, the way a web browser would.

Health information

What medicines you take, and anything you add to My health, is health information about you, and Australian law treats it as sensitive. We treat it that way too. It is stored against your account, and access through the app is limited to your account. Service providers process information as described in this policy. We do not sell health information or send it to pharmacies for marketing.

When you ask a question, the app looks through your saved health details and picks out the ones that bear on that question, for example a penicillin allergy when you ask about an antibiotic. When the question is about taking one of your own medicines, it can also include the other medicines you take, because that kind of question has to be looked at against all of them. What it picks is sent with your question to Anthropic, whose AI model writes the answer. The app does not save its own conclusions about your health as facts about you: only what you added, or agreed to save.

You can see, change or remove anything in My health at any time, from inside the app.

Answers we keep for a short time

When the app writes an answer it can keep it, so the same question does not have to be worked out twice.

  • An eligible answer that used your saved health details can be cached against your account only, for up to 48 hours. Only your account can be given it again.
  • A first answer that used nothing about you (no photograph, no health details, no cabinet and no earlier conversation) is kept with no account attached for up to 7 days. Somebody else who asks the same question about the same medicine in the same language can be given it.
  • A complete answer about a single medicine-pack photograph can be cached for your account only, never shared with another account. This cache expires five minutes after the answer completes, and reuse requires the photograph, question, context and source information to match. The photograph itself is not saved for this cache. Answers about other photographs are not cached this way.
  • Eligible exact conversation retries and questions without a linked source can also use a five-minute cache for the same account only. They are never shared between users.

These caches are separate from saved chat history. A question is converted to a code for cache lookup; saved chats separately retain the readable question and answer until you delete them. Reports built only from a medicine's public documents, and the spoken audio of sentences read from those documents, are also kept with no account attached, because they say nothing about anybody.

Sponsored placements and tracking

There are no advertising identifiers, no analytics SDKs and no tracking of you across other apps or websites. Nothing in this app is sold to a data broker. A sponsored pharmacy slot is the one paid placement in the app, it is labelled as one, and it is described above.

Deleting everything

You can delete your account from inside the app: Profile → Delete my account. That permanently removes your sign-in, your cabinet, your dose records, everything in My health, the photographs you kept, saved chats, your settings, your usage records and any answer cached against your account. You can also remove a single medicine or a single My health entry at any time, and removing a medicine deletes its dose records and any photograph you kept with it.

You do not have to give the AI permission to delete your account. If you have not given it, or you took it back, choose I do not allow this on the screen that asks for it. While you are signed in, the page that opens has Delete my account, Sign out and Manage subscription.

Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription. Apple takes the payment, so only you can cancel it, in your Apple account settings: Settings, your name, Subscriptions. Cancel first, or Apple keeps charging you. When the account is deleted the app asks RevenueCat to delete its record of you, and if you used Sign in with Apple it asks Apple to end the app's connection to your Apple Account. If either request does not go through, the deletion still happens, and you can remove the app yourself in your Apple account settings under Sign in with Apple. Apple keeps its own record of a purchase.

Some things are not removed, because they are not tied to you: an answer, a report or spoken audio kept with no account attached, what a pharmacy's web page said in a stock check, the count of times a sponsored slot was shown, and our log of purchase events, which is kept for 90 days with your account identifier taken off it. What a service provider already holds is deleted under that provider's own rules.

Two things are kept for 12 months after you delete your account, and then deleted. They are there so that deleting an account and making it again does not give out the free allowance twice, and so the spending limits all customers share stay correct. Neither holds your email address, your account identifier, a question, an answer or anything about your health.

  • A scrambled code made from your sign-in details, with two counts beside it: how many free reports and how many free questions the account had used. The code cannot be turned back into your email address.
  • The amounts your requests cost us to run, each with its date and the kind of request, under the same scrambled code.

If you sign up again with the same sign-in inside those 12 months, your free allowance carries on from where it was. It does not start again.
